# Vara — NSDR Audio Scripts
**Version 1.0 | April 2026 | Production-ready scripts for recording**

---

## Purpose

This document contains two complete, production-ready scripts for Vara's NSDR (Non-Sleep Deep Rest) protocol. Both scripts follow the same structural arc used in the most well-researched NSDR protocols, adapted for Vara's brand voice — calm, grounded, non-mystical, non-performative.

**Scripts included:**
- NSDR — 10 minutes (for midday reset and cognitive fatigue)
- NSDR — 20 minutes (for evening wind-down and deep recovery)

---

## Production notes (read before recording)

### Voice direction
- **Tone:** Calm but present. Not sleepy. Not whispered. Not performative. Think "trusted friend who happens to know this practice well."
- **Pace:** Slightly slower than conversational. About 120–140 words per minute. Allow full breaths between sentences.
- **Warmth:** Neutral-warm. Not overly soothing, which can feel patronizing. Not clinical, which can feel cold.
- **Age/gender:** Either masculine or feminine voice works. Choose one and commit.
- **Accent:** Neutral American English at launch. International expansion can add variants later.

### Pacing guidance
The scripts are marked with `[pause X sec]` instructions. These are **not suggestions** — they are critical for the protocol's effectiveness. NSDR works in the pause as much as in the words. Long pauses feel unnatural to record but are correct. Trust the silence.

### Recording technical specs
- **Format:** WAV master at 48kHz / 24-bit, deliver compressed MP3 at 192kbps for app use
- **Environment:** Acoustically treated room, pop filter, condenser microphone
- **Processing:** Light compression, gentle EQ, no reverb, no music bed
- **Silence:** Pauses should be true digital silence or very subtle room tone — NOT music, NOT nature sounds. Added audio textures distract from the protocol's mechanism.

### What NOT to include
- No background music or ambient nature sounds
- No intro/outro branding voiceover ("Welcome to Vara's NSDR practice...") — the app UI handles context
- No affirmations ("You are worthy. You are loved.")
- No spiritual or mystical language ("Your light body...", "Divine energy...", "Your higher self...")
- No promises of outcome ("You will feel completely restored...")
- No breathing count instructions during the body scan — breath should be natural, not paced
- No commands to visualize specific images (beaches, forests, etc.) — the body scan is sufficient

## SCRIPT 1: NSDR — 10 minutes

**Total runtime target:** 9:30–10:30 including pauses
**Use case:** Midday reset, post-meeting recovery, cognitive fatigue from sustained focus
**When it surfaces in Vara:** Recommended when user checks in as Foggy with less than 15 minutes available

---

### [OPENING — 0:00 to 0:45]

Find a comfortable position to lie down.

[pause 5 sec]

If lying flat isn't comfortable, a supported or slightly propped position is fine. What matters is that you won't need to adjust yourself for the next ten minutes.

[pause 4 sec]

Let your arms rest at your sides, palms open or resting however feels natural.

[pause 3 sec]

Close your eyes when you're ready.

[pause 6 sec]

---

### [SETTLING — 0:45 to 1:30]

Take one breath in through your nose.

[pause 3 sec]

And let it out slowly through your mouth.

[pause 4 sec]

Now let your breathing return to normal. You don't need to control it.

[pause 5 sec]

Just notice that you're breathing. Your body has been doing this all day without your attention. For the next few minutes, let it continue without your attention, too.

[pause 8 sec]

---

### [BODY SCAN — 1:30 to 7:30]

Bring your attention to the top of your head.

[pause 4 sec]

Notice any sensation there. Warmth. Tension. Stillness. Whatever is there, just notice it. You don't need to change it.

[pause 8 sec]

Let your attention move down to your forehead and the muscles around your eyes.

[pause 5 sec]

If there's tension there, see if it softens slightly as you notice it. No effort required.

[pause 8 sec]

Now your jaw.

[pause 4 sec]

The jaw holds a lot. Let it loosen, even slightly.

[pause 6 sec]

Move your attention to your neck and the top of your shoulders.

[pause 5 sec]

Notice the weight of your head resting wherever it's resting.

[pause 6 sec]

Let your attention move into your shoulders and down your arms. All the way to your hands.

[pause 8 sec]

Now your chest.

[pause 4 sec]

Feel the gentle rise and fall as you breathe. You're not changing it — just noticing.

[pause 8 sec]

Let your attention drop down to your belly.

[pause 5 sec]

The belly softens on its own when we notice it.

[pause 6 sec]

Now your hips and your lower back.

[pause 5 sec]

Feel the weight of your body settling into whatever is supporting you.

[pause 8 sec]

Let your attention flow down your legs. Your thighs, your knees, your calves.

[pause 6 sec]

All the way to your feet.

[pause 6 sec]

---

### [WHOLE-BODY AWARENESS — 7:30 to 9:00]

Now let your attention expand to include your whole body at once.

[pause 8 sec]

You don't need to hold any particular part in focus. Just a sense of your whole body, resting.

[pause 10 sec]

If your mind has wandered — and it will have — that's fine. Wandering is what minds do. Just return to the sense of your body, resting.

[pause 12 sec]

Your body knows how to rest. You're not doing anything. You're letting it happen.

[pause 15 sec]

---

### [RETURN — 9:00 to 10:00]

In a moment, you'll begin to return.

[pause 5 sec]

Start by bringing small movement back. Wiggle your fingers.

[pause 5 sec]

And your toes.

[pause 4 sec]

Take a slightly deeper breath.

[pause 4 sec]

And when you're ready — no rush — open your eyes.

[pause 6 sec]

You're done.

[END]

## SCRIPT 2: NSDR — 20 minutes

**Total runtime target:** 19:30–20:30 including pauses
**Use case:** Evening wind-down, full deep recovery, transition into sleep preparation
**When it surfaces in Vara:** Recommended for evening check-ins, in the Evening Wind-Down routine

---

### [OPENING — 0:00 to 1:30]

Find a comfortable position to lie down.

[pause 6 sec]

For this practice, flat on your back is usually best, but any position where you're fully supported is fine. What matters is that you can settle in without needing to adjust.

[pause 6 sec]

Let your arms rest wherever feels natural. Palms up, palms down — either is fine.

[pause 5 sec]

Your legs can be straight or slightly apart. If your lower back is tight, a pillow under your knees can help.

[pause 5 sec]

Close your eyes when you're ready.

[pause 10 sec]

---

### [SETTLING — 1:30 to 3:00]

Take one slow breath in through your nose.

[pause 4 sec]

And release it gently through your mouth.

[pause 5 sec]

Take one more breath in.

[pause 4 sec]

And exhale slowly.

[pause 6 sec]

Now let your breathing return to whatever is natural. You're not controlling it. You're not counting.

[pause 10 sec]

You've been in motion all day. Doing things. Holding things. For the next twenty minutes, there's nothing you need to do.

[pause 12 sec]

---

### [BODY SCAN — 3:00 to 14:00]

Bring your attention to the top of your head.

[pause 6 sec]

Notice whatever is there — pressure, warmth, tingling, stillness. You're not looking for anything specific. Just noticing what's already there.

[pause 10 sec]

Move your attention down to your forehead.

[pause 5 sec]

The space between your eyebrows.

[pause 6 sec]

The muscles around your eyes.

[pause 8 sec]

Your cheeks. Your jaw.

[pause 6 sec]

The jaw holds a lot of unconscious tension. Let it drop slightly, even if it already feels relaxed.

[pause 10 sec]

Bring your attention to your mouth. Your tongue, resting in your mouth.

[pause 6 sec]

Your throat.

[pause 8 sec]

Now your neck, and the back of your head.

[pause 6 sec]

Feel where your head is supported. The weight of it, fully resting.

[pause 10 sec]

Let your attention move into your shoulders.

[pause 5 sec]

Shoulders often hold the shape of a day — holding, reaching, bracing. Let them soften.

[pause 10 sec]

Move your attention down your upper arms.

[pause 5 sec]

Your elbows.

[pause 5 sec]

Your forearms.

[pause 6 sec]

Your wrists and your hands.

[pause 8 sec]

All the way to your fingertips.

[pause 8 sec]

Now bring your attention to your chest.

[pause 5 sec]

Notice the gentle rise and fall of your breath. You're not changing it, just noticing.

[pause 10 sec]

Let your attention move to your upper back, the area between your shoulder blades.

[pause 8 sec]

And down into your ribs.

[pause 6 sec]

Your middle back, supporting you.

[pause 10 sec]

Move your attention to your belly.

[pause 5 sec]

The belly softens on its own when we notice it without trying to change anything.

[pause 10 sec]

Now your lower back and your hips.

[pause 6 sec]

Feel the weight of your pelvis settling, resting fully.

[pause 10 sec]

Let your attention flow down the front of your thighs.

[pause 6 sec]

Your knees.

[pause 6 sec]

Your calves.

[pause 6 sec]

Your ankles.

[pause 6 sec]

The tops of your feet.

[pause 6 sec]

The soles of your feet.

[pause 6 sec]

All the way to your toes.

[pause 10 sec]

---

### [WHOLE-BODY AWARENESS — 14:00 to 18:00]

Now let your attention expand to include your whole body at once.

[pause 10 sec]

You're not holding any particular part in focus. Just a sense of your whole body, fully supported, fully resting.

[pause 15 sec]

You're awake, but deeply at rest.

[pause 15 sec]

If your mind wanders — and it will — that's fine. Wandering is what minds do. Gently return to the sense of your whole body, resting.

[pause 15 sec]

Your body knows how to rest. You don't need to make it happen. You're letting it happen.

[pause 20 sec]

Notice what it feels like, to not be doing anything.

[pause 25 sec]

There's nothing to fix. Nothing to work on. Just this.

[pause 30 sec]

---

### [RETURN — 18:00 to 20:00]

In a moment, you'll begin to return.

[pause 8 sec]

Take your time.

[pause 5 sec]

Start by bringing small movements back to your body. Wiggle your fingers.

[pause 6 sec]

And your toes.

[pause 5 sec]

Roll your shoulders slightly, or whatever small movement feels right.

[pause 8 sec]

Take a slightly deeper breath.

[pause 5 sec]

And another.

[pause 6 sec]

When you're ready — and only when you're ready — open your eyes.

[pause 8 sec]

Come back slowly. There's no rush.

[pause 8 sec]

You're done.

[END]

## Version notes and alternates

### Script variants to consider post-launch
Once you have launch data showing which durations users prefer, consider adding:
- **5-minute version** for acute midday cognitive fatigue (would require significant pacing rework, not just trimming)
- **30-minute version** for longer deep-rest sessions (natural extension of the 20-minute with more body scan depth)

Don't add these at launch. Two variants is the right starting point. More variants means more production cost and more maintenance when you iterate.

### What to avoid in variants
- Specialized "themed" NSDR (sleep NSDR, anxiety NSDR, focus NSDR) — these fragment the protocol and require separate recordings
- Celebrity guest recordings — creates brand dependency
- Music-overlay versions — if you want ambient audio, it should be a separate feature, not a variant

### Lineage and credit
The NSDR protocol is public-domain as a method. The specific language, pacing, and structure of these scripts is original to Vara and copyright Vara Wellness. Internally, this protocol draws on the yoga nidra tradition (originally documented by Satyananda Saraswati in the 20th century, though the method is older) and the modernization work of Andrew Huberman, Stephen Porges, and others. None of that source material needs to be credited in the recording or UI — it's the method that's public, not any specific recording.

## Implementation in the app

### File delivery
The recorded audio files should be stored in Vara's CDN and streamed or downloaded per user session.
- Launch with two files: `nsdr_10min_v1.mp3` and `nsdr_20min_v1.mp3`
- Version the filenames so re-recordings don't break existing installs
- Expect file sizes around 14MB (10-min) and 29MB (20-min) at 192kbps MP3

### Player UI
The NSDR protocol is one of the few where the running screen differs meaningfully from the breath and sensory protocols. It needs:
- Duration indicator with current position and total runtime
- Pause/resume
- Skip back 15 seconds (in case user zoned out and wants to catch a line)
- Volume control
- "End early" option with confirmation ("Are you sure? We'll still save this session.")

Do NOT include scrubbing/seeking through the audio. NSDR is a linear practice; letting users jump around defeats the mechanism.

### First-time orientation for NSDR
Because NSDR requires committing 10–20 minutes of inactive time, the first-time orientation is particularly important. Emphasize:
- You'll need to lie down
- You'll need a quiet space
- You won't need to do anything active
- It's normal for your mind to wander
