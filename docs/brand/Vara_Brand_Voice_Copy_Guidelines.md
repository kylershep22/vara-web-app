# Vara — Brand Voice & Copy Guidelines

> **Status: current source of truth for Vara's brand voice and in-app copy, as of August 2026.**
> Supersedes Voice & Tone v2.0 / v2.1 / v2.2 and, where they conflict, the copy-relevant guardrails
> of the Canonical Positioning Brief, the Brand One-Pager, and Mobile UI Standards §7.8.
> **What changed vs those docs:** this guide permits *earned recognition* of consistency, firsts,
> milestones, and returning (see §0.5), including neutral consistency counts. It still bans streak
> *pressure*, guilt, deficit framing, and loss-aversion or countdown mechanics. Reconciling the three
> older docs to match this one is a logged task, not yet done. Until it is, this doc wins on any
> celebration or progress-framing conflict.

## Revised voice direction

Vara should feel supportive without sounding soft, therapeutic, or overly accommodating.

The user is here because they want something to improve. Vara should help them work with reality, take meaningful action, and feel good about genuine progress.

The tone is:

- calm, but not sleepy
- encouraging, but not cheerleading
- direct, but not harsh
- adaptive, but not permissive
- positive about progress, without creating pressure

A good Vara line should sound like something a thoughtful person would actually say.

## 0. Global voice anchors

### 0.1 North stars

1. Where are you starting from today?
2. Pick one thing worth doing.
3. You've kept this going. That's real progress.

### 0.2 Worst offenders

1. Stay on track.
2. Complete your goals for today.
3. Track your progress.

These sound generic and transactional. Vara should help the user make progress, not simply report whether they did.

### 0.3 The signature move

Say what's happening plainly, then help the user do something about it.

Vara notices context without dramatizing it, reduces the number of decisions in front of the user, and points toward a practical next step.

### 0.4 Bans

- streak pressure, guilt, or deficit framing
- optimize / optimization
- attention-as-currency language
- em dashes in UI copy
- red except coral for true errors
- reflecting a named derived state back after the work
- scores, percentages, readiness indices, or composite grades unless the user explicitly asks for them
- hustle, grind, or productivity-as-worth language
- forced positivity
- therapy-speak
- overly soothing language
- hollow praise such as "Amazing!" or "Great job!" with no connection to what the user did
- fake intimacy or language that sounds like Vara knows the user better than they know themselves
- self-help clichés such as "best self," "unlock your potential," and "crush your goals"

### 0.5 Progress and celebration rule

Progress should be recognized, not weaponized.

Vara can celebrate:

- consistency
- meaningful streaks
- firsts
- milestones
- returning to something
- doing something difficult
- increasing effort or capability over time

Good:

- You've kept this going. That's real progress.
- First full week. Nice work.
- That used to be hard to make time for. Now it's becoming part of your week.
- You stuck with it today.
- A month in. You've built something here.

Avoid:

- Don't lose your streak!
- You missed yesterday.
- Only two more days to keep your streak alive.
- Get back on track.

Missing a day does not erase previous progress.

## 1. Daily loop core

### 1.1 Pre-pick hero

**Headline:** Where are you starting from today?

**Subline:** Quick check-in, then Vara will suggest a good place to start.

### 1.2 Capacity tiers

- **Normal:** Ready to make some progress.
- **Limited:** Some room, so be selective.
- **Slammed:** Very little room. Keep the bar realistic.

### 1.3 Time question

**Prompt:** How much time can you realistically give this?

**Chips:** 5 minutes / 10 to 15 / 20+

### 1.4 Picked state

Here's where to start.

### 1.5 Completion pattern

**Button:** Mark it done

**After a short practice:** Nice. You made the time.

**After a bigger effort:** Solid work. You stayed with it.

Additional progress acknowledgments CC can extend from:

- First one done. That's how this starts.
- You've kept this going. That's real progress.
- That was a bigger effort than usual. Nice work.
- You came back to it today. That counts.
- This is starting to look like something you actually do.

### 1.6 Scheduled state

Set for 3:00. We'll remind you then.

Use the actual scheduled time dynamically.

### 1.7 Skip

Not now

Return to the original invitation unchanged. No explanation, guilt, or interpretation.

## 2. Daily check-in

### 2.1 Situation prompt + labels

**Header:** What do you need most today?

Options:

1. Clear my head
2. Get some energy back
3. Wind down
4. Get my day under control
5. Reset for a few minutes
6. Deal with something hard

### 2.2 State tap 1

**Prompt:** Where's your energy?

**Options:** High / Low

### 2.3 State tap 2

**Prompt:** How does that feel?

**Options:** Good / Not great

### 2.4 Derived-state rule

The derived quadrant is internal routing logic only.

Do not label it back to the user, summarize it, score it, or turn it into an identity statement.

---

## 3. Post-session reflection

**Prompt:** Any change?

**Options:** A lot / A little / Not really

**After:** Leave blank by default.

The app does not need to congratulate the user for answering the question, and the options should not force them to say the practice worked.

## 4. Onboarding

> **HOLD — this section describes a five-question flow. The live V3 arc is eight screens

> and contains none of Q3, Q4, or Q5 below. Do not write onboarding copy from this section
> until it is re-specced against the real arc. The strings below are retained as voice
> reference only, not as a screen map.**

### Intro

A few quick questions so Vara can be more useful from the start.

### Q1

What would make the biggest difference in your life right now?

Options:

- Focus more easily
- Have more energy
- Feel more in control of my time
- Build better habits
- Feel less overwhelmed
- Something else

### Q2

Instead of reflecting the user's pain back to them, use this moment to understand the reason behind the change:

Why does that matter to you?

Open response.

This answer can become part of Vara's longer-term personalization, including goals, habits, recommendations, and AI coaching.

### Q3

When does it usually get hardest?

Options: Morning / Middle of the day / Evening

### Q4

How much time can you usually give this?

Options: 5 minutes / 10 to 15 / 20+

### Q5

What's been getting in the way lately?

Open response.

## 5. Nudges and notifications

### Work-start

Want to clear your head before the day gets moving?

### Evening

Ready to wind things down?

### Additional

If your focus is all over the place, take five and reset.

Got a few minutes? Use them for something that moves you forward.

Notification voice should be present-focused and useful. It should not mention what the user failed to do, how long it has been, or what they should have done already.

## 6. Return after absence

**Headline:** Good to have you back.

**Line:** No catching up. Start with today.

**CTA:** Start today

The user should feel welcomed without Vara pretending the absence never happened or making them feel guilty about it.

## 8. One noticing per surface

Examples:

- Mornings seem to be when this works best for you.
- You've been choosing the longer sessions more often lately.
- You've been coming back to this consistently.
- You're spending more time on the habits you said mattered most.

Rules:

- observation, not judgment
- qualitative unless the user intentionally opens a stats or progress view
- no diagnosis
- no "we know you better than you know yourself" tone
- patterns may recognize improvement
- do not turn every observation into advice

## 9. Systemic strings

- **Network error:** Something didn't connect. Try again.
- **Empty state:** No reflections yet. Add your first one.
- **Loading after 3s:** Still working on it.
- **Confirmation:** Saved. / Reflection saved.
- **Disabled tooltip:** Finish the step above first.

System copy should usually be shorter and more direct than coaching copy.

## Principles for CC

1. **Sound like a person.** Prefer plain language over polished wellness language.
2. **Progress matters.** Vara exists to help people make meaningful improvements in their lives. Do not flatten that ambition in the name of being supportive.
3. **Support without over-soothing.** The user does not need to be reassured in every interaction.
4. **Adapt the plan, not the goal.** A hard day may change the size or type of action Vara recommends, but it does not mean the user is incapable of making progress.
5. **Recognize earned wins.** Consistency, firsts, milestones, effort, and follow-through deserve specific acknowledgment.
6. **Never turn progress into pressure.** A streak is evidence of consistency, not something the user owes the app tomorrow.
7. **Be direct.** "Try this," "start here," and "mark it done" are often better than heavily softened alternatives.
8. **Reduce decisions.** Vara should make the next step clearer, not create another layer of choices.
9. **Notice without judging.** Patterns are information, not grades.
10. **Keep intelligence in the background.** Personalization can be sophisticated without the copy sounding clever or uncanny.
11. **Avoid therapy voice.** Vara can be warm without sounding clinical, therapeutic, or emotionally over-attuned.
12. **Avoid AI rhythm.** Do not overuse balanced constructions, metaphors, three-part phrases, rhetorical contrasts, or lines that sound written for a brand deck rather than spoken by a person.
13. **Only replace placeholders that map to an approved slot or pattern.** Unmapped gaps should be flagged.
14. **Do not fill protocol content, educational claims, efficacy claims, or "why this works" copy** without the appropriate reviewed source.
15. **Zero em dashes in UI copy.**
16. **Run the human test.** Read every line aloud. If it sounds like brand copy instead of something a real person would naturally say, rewrite it.
